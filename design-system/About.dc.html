The moment a model has to survive contact with physics.

My bachelor thesis, "Complex Time Series Forecasting in Public Flight Operations", was written at Fraunhofer IAIS inside the EU Horizon 2020 project DatAcron, predicting the behaviour of physical systems from messy multimodal telemetry.

That thesis set the pattern for everything since: I'm interested in the moment where a model stops being a benchmark number and has to survive contact with physics.

03 — Why Homunculus exists

Robotics is gated. We deleted the gate.

For the last stretch I've been deep in the field: the KI-Schmiede accelerator, a robotics network spanning Cologne, Bonn, Berlin, Zurich and Munich, and a lot of hours on factory floors talking to German SMEs about why they don't buy robots.

The answer is almost never "the robot can't do it." It's the software overhead, the integration cost, the fact that every new task needs an engineer who already has a six-month waiting list.

Meanwhile the models got good. VLAs and world models are solving single skills faster than anyone expected. What's missing isn't intelligence. It's a place to play — a low-friction surface where the first ten thousand people who could invent a useful machine actually get to try.

So: idealized modules today, real arms and imported 3D models next. Snap parts together, hit go, watch your invention teach itself to walk, crawl, roll or flail. The flailing is part of it.

04 — Things I keep doing

Winning hackathons on purpose

At the FORGIS × Arke × IBM × Google DeepMind × {Tech: Europe} Physical AI Hackathon in Zurich, my team took 1st place in the IBM track and 1st place overall. We built a document-to-action pipeline: IBM Docling parsed real production PDFs, a Gemini agent retrieved grounded evidence and composed executable sub-steps, and a 3D vision pipeline with camera localisation and inverse-kinematics planning drove an SO-ARM100 6-axis arm through a real industrial test — measuring the 5V rail on an Arduino board, purely from documentation it had read itself. 05 — What I believe
01

Robots should be inventable, not just purchasable.

The bottleneck in robotics is not motors. It's access.

02

Simulation is the cheapest place to be wrong.

Ten thousand failed designs in a browser tab beat one expensive prototype.

03

The hard part is orchestration.

Individual skills are getting solved. Chaining them into something that finishes a job is where the value sits.

04

Hardware never matches the datasheet.

Sensors drift, actuators run a few percent fast. Anything I build leaves room for calibration, because the physical world doesn't care about your clean abstraction.

05

Design matters early.

We're about to fill the world with a whole new category of object. Grey boxes are a choice, and a bad one.

06 — The name

A homunculus is the little artificial being alchemists claimed to grow in a flask — an entire creature, assembled from parts, brought to life by someone patient enough to try. Also: the cortical homunculus, the distorted map of the body in your motor cortex, all giant hands and lips, drawn in proportion to how much brain it takes to move each part.

Both meanings are the product. Assemble a body from parts. Let it grow its own motor map.
